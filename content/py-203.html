Practice: Printing Variables (Comma & Plus Only)

Rule: Do not use f"..." f-strings. Use only commas (,), plus (+), and str() where needed. You MUST print EXACTLY how the questions ask you to print.

Question 01 Greeting with punctuation

Create a variable called name with value "Tom" and print the exact text below:

Expected Output Hello, Tom!
💡 Step 1: Need a clue?
Remember that a comma adds a space before ! (giving Hello, Tom !). Use + to glue the exclamation mark directly to name with no space.
✅ Step 2: Still stuck? Show answer & explanation 
name = "Tom"
print("Hello,", name + "!")

+ adds no space, making it ideal for attaching punctuation like name + "!", while the comma handles the space after "Hello,".

Question 02 Age sentence

Create a variable age with value 10 and print the exact text below:

Expected Output I am 10 years old.
💡 Step 1: Need a clue?
Since there are normal spaces around the number 10, you can separate the three parts with commas—or convert age with str() if using +.
✅ Step 2: Still stuck? Show answer & explanation 
age = 10
print("I am", age, "years old.")
# Or using +:
print("I am " + str(age) + " years old.")

Commas accept numbers directly and add spaces automatically. If you used +, wrapping age in str(age) prevents a TypeError.

Question 03 Math equation

Create two variables a = 4 and b = 6 and print the exact text below:

Expected Output 4 + 6 = 10
💡 Step 1: Need a clue?
Every symbol (+ and =) has spaces around it. Pass a, "+", b, "=", and a + b as separate items to print().
✅ Step 2: Still stuck? Show answer & explanation

b = 6
print(a, "+", b, "=", a + b)

+ between two integers (a + b) performs math (10), while separating each piece with commas adds the exact single spaces needed around + and =.

Question 04 Price tag

Question 08 Coordinates

Create variables x = 3 and y = 1.5 and print the exact text below:

Expected Output x is 3, y is 1.5
💡 Step 1: Need a clue?
Watch out for the comma right after 3! Putting a comma inside print("x is", x, ",") prints 3 , with an unwanted space before the comma.
✅ Step 2: Still stuck? Show answer & explanation 
x = 3
y = 1.5
print("x is " + str(x) + ", y is " + str(y))
# Or mixing comma and plus:
print("x is", str(x) + ", y is", y)

Attaching , directly after 3 with no space requires +, which in turn requires converting x with str(x).
